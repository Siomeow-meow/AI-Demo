# POS Lesson: Point-of-Sale Basics

A point-of-sale (POS) system is where a sale is rung up and paid for. It combines hardware, such as a terminal or tablet, barcode scanner, receipt printer, cash drawer and card reader, with software that holds the product catalogue and prices, records sales, applies discounts and tax, and updates stock.

Cloud POS stores data online and can combine many shops in one view; it needs internet, so good systems have an offline mode that syncs later. Local POS runs on a store computer.

A typical sale: scan items, apply any discount, calculate tax, take payment (cash, card, e-wallet or split), print or send the receipt, and deduct stock. Connecting the POS to inventory and accounting avoids typing the same data twice.

Useful reports: daily sales by hour, best and worst sellers, sales by cashier, refunds and voids, and stock levels.
