# General Lesson: Data Literacy

Averages hide detail. The mean is pulled by extreme values; the median is the middle value and is more robust when data is skewed (like salaries). Always check the spread too, not only the centre.

Correlation is not causation: two things moving together does not mean one causes the other; there may be a hidden third factor or pure coincidence. Experiments, such as A/B tests, are needed to prove cause.

Watch for bias: a sample that is small or not representative (only people who answered a survey) can mislead. Survivorship bias looks only at the successes. Percent changes can mislead when the base is small, so look at absolute numbers as well.

Charts: start bar charts at zero, label axes and units, choose the chart type to match the question (lines for trends, bars for comparison), and do not use 3D effects.

Before trusting a number, ask: where did the data come from, how was it collected, what period does it cover, what is missing, and how was the metric defined?
