# Finance Lesson: Accounting Basics

Double-entry bookkeeping records every transaction in at least two accounts so that total debits always equal total credits. Debit means the left side of an account and credit means the right side. Assets and expenses increase with debits; liabilities, equity and revenue increase with credits.

Example: selling goods for 1,000 on credit. Debit Accounts Receivable 1,000 and credit Sales Revenue 1,000. When the customer pays, debit Cash and credit Accounts Receivable.

The chart of accounts is the list of all accounts, usually numbered by type (assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, expenses). The general ledger holds the balances, and a trial balance lists them all to check that debits equal credits.

Cash accounting records income and expenses when money moves; accrual accounting records them when earned or incurred, which gives a truer picture of performance. Most businesses of any size use accrual accounting.
