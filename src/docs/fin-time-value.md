# Finance Lesson: Time Value of Money

Money today is worth more than the same amount later, because it can earn interest and inflation erodes purchasing power.

Future value: FV = PV x (1 + r)^n. Present value: PV = FV / (1 + r)^n. Here r is the rate per period and n is the number of periods. 10,000 at 6% a year for 10 years grows to about 17,908.

Compounding more often (monthly instead of yearly) gives slightly more. The rule of 72 estimates doubling time: years to double is about 72 divided by the interest rate in percent, so at 8% it takes about 9 years.

For investments, NPV (net present value) discounts all future cash flows to today and subtracts the cost; a positive NPV adds value. IRR is the discount rate that makes NPV zero. Payback is how long it takes to recover the investment.

For loans, a fixed-rate amortizing loan has equal payments where the early payments are mostly interest and later ones mostly principal.
