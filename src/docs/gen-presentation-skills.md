# General Lesson: Presenting Clearly

Start with the audience: what do they need to decide or understand? Build the talk around one main message and three or fewer supporting points. A simple structure is: why it matters, what we found, what to do next.

Slides support you; they should not be your script. Use one idea per slide, few words, large text and clear visuals. Replace dense tables with a single chart that shows the point, and put detail in an appendix.

Delivery: practise out loud and time yourself, speak slower than feels natural, pause after key points, make eye contact, and use stories or examples to make numbers memorable. Open with the conclusion for busy managers.

Questions: listen to the whole question, repeat it if the room did not hear it, answer briefly, and say "I will find out and follow up" rather than guessing. Test the equipment and the room before you start.
