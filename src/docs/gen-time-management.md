# General Lesson: Time Management

The Eisenhower matrix sorts tasks by urgent and important: do important and urgent tasks now, schedule important but not urgent ones, delegate urgent but not important ones, and drop the rest. Most long-term progress comes from the important but not urgent group, so protect time for it.

Time blocking puts tasks in your calendar rather than only on a list. Group similar tasks, and schedule hard work for the time of day when you are most alert. The two-minute rule says that if a task takes under two minutes, do it now.

Plan the day the evening before with three main tasks. Estimate honestly and add buffer, since tasks usually take longer than expected. Say no, or negotiate deadlines, when your plate is full.

Reduce context switching: batch email checks instead of reacting to each message, and keep long focus blocks free from meetings and notifications. Review your week briefly each Friday to see what worked.
