# Business Lesson: Choosing KPIs

A KPI (key performance indicator) is a number that shows whether you are reaching an important goal. Pick a few that matter, rather than tracking everything.

Lagging indicators report results after they happen, such as revenue or customer churn. Leading indicators predict results, such as number of sales calls or website visits. Use both.

A good KPI is specific, measurable, owned by a person, tied to a goal and reviewed on a regular schedule. Always define the formula, data source and time period, or two people will calculate it differently.

Examples by area: Sales (conversion rate, average order value), Finance (gross margin, days sales outstanding), Operations (on-time delivery, inventory turnover), Customer (NPS, churn, first response time), People (turnover, utilization).

Beware of vanity metrics, such as page views, that look good but do not connect to decisions.
