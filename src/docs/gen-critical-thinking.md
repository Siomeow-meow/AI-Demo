# General Lesson: Critical Thinking

Ask of any claim: who says it, what is their evidence, what might they gain, and is there a simpler explanation? Prefer primary sources and several independent sources over a single article or post.

Common logical fallacies: ad hominem (attacking the person rather than the argument), straw man (misrepresenting a view to attack it), false dilemma (only two options offered), appeal to authority (it is true because an expert said so, without evidence), slippery slope, and post hoc (B followed A, so A caused B).

Cognitive biases: confirmation bias means we favour information that agrees with us; anchoring means the first number we see affects later judgement. Slow down on important decisions, look for evidence that could prove you wrong, and consider base rates (how often something happens in general).

Write down the decision, the reasons and what would change your mind. Separate facts from opinions and assumptions, and test the key assumptions.
