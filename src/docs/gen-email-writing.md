# General Lesson: Writing Clear Work Emails

Subject line: say what it is and what you need, such as "Approval needed by Friday: Q4 supplier contract".

Structure: a greeting, the main point or request in the first two lines, the context the reader needs, any deadline, and a clear next step. Use short paragraphs, bullets for lists and bold sparingly. Put the most important thing first.

Tone: polite and direct. Avoid long apologies, sarcasm and writing in capitals. Read it once as the recipient would. Check names, dates, numbers and attachments before sending. Use reply-all only when everyone needs the answer.

Example: "Hi Maria, could you approve the attached leave request for John by Thursday? He is out 3 days from Monday and the handover is done. Thanks, Ana."

If something is sensitive or complicated, a short call may be better than a long email thread. Follow up politely if you have no response by the deadline.
