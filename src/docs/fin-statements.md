# Finance Lesson: The Three Financial Statements

The income statement (profit and loss) shows performance over a period: revenue minus cost of goods sold gives gross profit; minus operating expenses gives operating income; after interest and tax you reach net income.

The balance sheet is a snapshot at one date. It follows the accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Assets are what the company owns (cash, receivables, inventory, equipment), liabilities are what it owes (payables, loans), and equity is the owners' stake.

The cash flow statement explains the change in cash through operating, investing and financing activities. A company can be profitable and still run out of cash, for example when customers pay late or too much money is tied up in inventory.

The statements connect: net income flows into equity on the balance sheet, and the cash flow statement reconciles profit to the change in the cash balance.
