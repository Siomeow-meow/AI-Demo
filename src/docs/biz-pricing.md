# Business Lesson: Pricing Strategies

Cost-plus pricing adds a markup to your cost. It is simple but ignores what customers will pay. Value-based pricing sets the price from the value the customer gets. Competitive pricing follows the market. Penetration pricing starts low to win share; skimming starts high and lowers over time.

Before choosing a price, know your costs: variable costs per unit and fixed costs. The break-even point equals fixed costs divided by (price - variable cost per unit).

Price elasticity describes how much demand changes when price changes. Essentials are usually inelastic; luxuries and items with many substitutes are elastic.

Tips: test prices on small groups, offer good-better-best tiers, avoid frequent discounting that trains customers to wait for sales, and review pricing at least once a year or when costs change.
